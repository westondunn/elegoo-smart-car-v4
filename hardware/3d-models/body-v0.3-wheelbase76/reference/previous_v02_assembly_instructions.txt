ELEGOO V4 — REMOVABLE FULL BODY FOR THE EXPLORER
Version 0.2.0 | 12 October 2026 | Millimeters | PLA prototype

START HERE

Open elegoo_v4_full_body_assembly.step in Fusion to inspect the complete
rover with its new body. Print body_00_joint_check.3mf first to check your
PLA hole fit and the retained roof-nut channel before printing large panels.

This add-on consists of SIX main body pieces:
  1. Left side shell with wheel arches and ventilation.
  2. Right side shell with wheel arches and connector access.
  3. Removable rear panel with battery and rear-sensor openings.
  4. Front bulkhead with a cable opening behind the camera/sonar head.
  5. Removable vented roof with four retained nuts and a generic mount grid.
  6. Low front cowl around the servo head, guard and downward sensor mounts.

The original bottom plate remains the floor of the rover. Open wheel arches,
vents, connector passages and the exposed camera/sonar head are intentional.
The shell provides body coverage and removable service panels; it is not
weather sealed or impact-rated.


HOW IT FITS THE EARLIER EXPLORER KIT

The body replaces the two short side guards from the earlier design. It uses
their existing mounting holes in the slotted deck; no new chassis holes are
specified. The earlier source-derived chassis and hardware layout are retained.

Required earlier printed parts:
  01_slotted_deck.stl                 1
  02_deck_spacer_36mm.stl              4
  05_front_guard.stl                  1
  06_front_guard_spacer_4mm.stl        2

The optional Pi tray, rear sensor carrier, floor-facing sensor carriers and
servo adapter can remain installed. The assembly shows these optional items
as reference geometry, not as requirements for using the shell.

If you have not fitted the original deck yet, use the earlier mounting gauge
on its four 36 mm spacers first. The new joint samples check the printed
body connections; they do not verify the car's mounting-hole positions.

The main body has a 142 mm outside width. The cabin extends from Y=-120 to
Y=46 mm; the low front cowl continues to Y=120.5 mm. Skirts stop at Z=20 mm.
The roof underside is Z=144 mm, with 2.8 mm roof thickness. Main walls are
2.4 mm thick. All positions use the original rover coordinates: +Y forward,
+Z upward, and nominal ground at Z=0.

PRINT SIZE AND STARTING SETTINGS

Design                     Print-oriented dimensions (X x Y x Z, mm)
Left side                  124.0 x 166.0 x 21.0
Right side                 124.0 x 166.0 x 21.0
Rear panel                 136.2 x 124.0 x 2.4
Front bulkhead             136.2 x 75.795 x 2.4
Roof                       142.0 x 166.0 x 2.8
Front cowl                 142.0 x 73.9 x 57.105
Joint side sample           22.0 x 24.0 x 19.0
Joint rear sample           16.1 x 22.0 x 2.4

Every design fits within 180 x 180 mm with a 4 mm brim. The largest
individual XY dimension is 166 mm, or 174 mm including that brim.
The 3MF layouts have 4 mm edge margins; the two samples also have at least
5 mm spacing. Check the selected printer's actual usable area in the slicer.

Suggested initial setup, not a physically tested printer profile:
  - PLA with your established temperature and flow settings.
  - 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.20 mm layers, four walls.
  - Approximately 20-25% infill and four or five top/bottom layers.
  - Consider a 4 mm brim on the large flat panels.
  - Side/end panels print with their broad outer faces down.
  - Roof prints outside face down. The cowl prints bonnet-face down,
    with the skirts growing upward; this avoids a large suspended bonnet.
  - Inspect the nut channels, mounting-tab holes and short bridges in the
    sliced preview. Add local supports only where the selected settings
    need them, and keep the nut channels clean enough to fit a metal nut.

Use the slicer's material estimate for the actual PLA quantity. Thin walls,
top/bottom layers and infill make CAD solid volume different from filament
use. Added body mass should be included when tuning speed and acceleration.


FASTENERS

Additional hardware for the six body panels:
  8 x M3 x 12 mm screws      Front/rear panels to side-panel corner tabs.
  4 x M3 x 10 mm screws      Removable roof.
  12 x M3 hex nuts           Eight end-panel nuts and four roof nuts.

Retained from the earlier explorer assembly:
  4 x M3 x 10 mm + four nuts Side-panel tabs to the existing deck.
  2 x M3 x 20 mm             Front guard/cowl to the front pillars.
  4 x M3 x 50 mm             Original deck/spacer stack; unchanged.

Screw lengths are measured under the head. Check your actual hardware,
thread depth and access before tightening. The modeled M3 nut is about
5.5 mm across flats and 2.4 mm thick. The roof insertion channel is 5.9 mm
wide and 2.8 mm high; the hex pocket is 6.8 mm across corners.

The front cowl adds 2.4 mm on top of the guard arms. Reusing the M3 x 20 mm
screws gives approximately 5.1 mm nominal engagement in the front pillars
at the modeled stack. Do not add arbitrary washers or a different cowl
thickness without recalculating that engagement and checking real threads.

End panels seat directly on their corner tabs. The 0.5 mm seam allowance is
between perimeter edges; there is no designed gap in the clamped screw stack.
The body uses metal hardware and clearance holes, with no printed threads
or repeated-flexure PLA catches.


ASSEMBLY ORDER

1. Print both joint samples. Slide an M3 nut outward into the sample roof
   channel until it reaches the hex pocket. It should enter without forcing
   or splitting the PLA. Join the two samples with an M3 x 12 screw and nut
   to check the main panel-hole fit. Reuse that hardware in the final body.

2. Power off the rover. Confirm the earlier deck/spacers/front guard are
   fitted correctly. Remove the original short side guards, if installed.

3. Fit each long side shell around the wheels and bolt its two local tabs
   beneath the deck at X=+/-57, Y=-62/-28. Reuse four M3 x 10 screws/nuts.
   Keep the end panels and roof off while handling those nuts. A small
   piece of removable tape can temporarily hold a nut during assembly;
   the screw/nut joint carries the load.

4. Slide the four roof nuts into their side-panel channels from inside the
   cabin toward the outside. Their centers are X=+/-61, Y=-104/+30. Seat
   them in the hex pockets. These channels restrain rotation and vertical
   loss of the nuts; they remain open toward the interior for replacement.

5. Fit the rear panel and front bulkhead using four M3 x 12 screws/nuts
   each. Hold the nuts from above while the roof is still off. Route the
   head cable through the front opening and leave enough travel for turning.

6. Remove the two front-guard/pillar screws, leaving the guard and its two
   4 mm shims in position. Lower the cowl over the head through its 62 mm
   opening and onto the guard arms, then reinstall the same M3 x 20 screws
   through the cowl. The modeled head fits through this opening, but check
   your actual camera/brackets and loosen them temporarily if needed.

7. Check that the four rounded open notches clear the existing floor-sensor
   carrier screw heads. The lower front passages also clear the guard arms
   and the two carriers. Do not trap any wires in these openings.

8. Install the roof last with four M3 x 10 screws into the retained nuts.
   The roof can subsequently be removed independently. Keep the car upright
   during service so loose components stay in their intended positions.

9. Turn each wheel by hand and move the head through its intended range with
   the real cables connected. Confirm no rubbing or snagging and check the
   live camera image and distance readings near the limits of the scan.

10. Before a long autonomous run, check operating temperatures with the roof
    fitted and verify that body weight has not introduced wheel slip or
    unreliable starts/stops. The digital model does not establish thermal
    performance or motor payload capacity.

Tighten by hand only enough to seat the parts. If a nut channel or mounting
hole prints too tight, adjust that local feature after testing the sample.
Do not scale the whole body to correct an individual fit issue.


ACCESS AND FUTURE ADDITIONS

The right side includes generous nominal UNO and Pi connector passages.
The rear has separate battery/switch and rear-sensor openings. The removable
roof gives access to the computer, controller and wiring. These openings
were checked against modeled connectors and straight approach volumes;
unmodeled cable plugs and bend radii may still require changes.

The roof leaves about 8.7 mm above the highest modeled Pi connector. This
does not establish clearance for a HAT, case, tall cooler or upward-routed
cable. Adjust the roof height and revalidate the design for those additions.

A generic 40 x 20 mm M3-clearance grid is included toward the front of the
roof at X=+/-20 and Y=-5/+15. It can support a future lightweight adapter
plate for a beacon or sensor. It is not a verified RPLIDAR mounting pattern,
and roof stiffness/payload capacity has not been load-tested.

The camera/sonar head remains physically exposed. The body clears its rigid
rotation envelope; this alone does not prove that every peripheral camera
ray or ultrasonic beam is clear. Check the actual camera view and sensing
response with the physical body installed before using those observations
for mapping. Record measured sensor transforms after final assembly.

SOURCE, REBUILDING AND TRACEABILITY

Keep the following files together:
  build_full_body.py
  full_body_parameters.json
  body_reference_rover.py
  body_reference_parameters.json
  body_reference_profiles.json
  rover_3mf.py
  render_full_body.py
  requirements.txt

The body_reference files are a self-contained copy of the earlier rover
generator/data with local dependency filenames adjusted. They preserve the
source-derived chassis and earlier reference geometry. The new body builder
filters out the two old guards and replaces the two guard-screw positions;
the original delivered files remain usable as the open-body configuration.

In a Python 3.11+ environment:
  python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
  python build_full_body.py --output rebuilt
  python build_full_body.py --output rebuilt --render

Validate rebuilt export files:
  python validate_body_exports.py --deliverables rebuilt --report validation.json

Validate the current source model geometry:
  python review_body_geometry.py --model-dir . --output fit_review.json

body_manifest.json records all components, evidence labels, part quantities,
interfaces, expected exports and source hashes. SHA256SUMS.txt covers the
delivered files. The retained-nut report and preview QA report include their
reviewed source/image identities.

Major body settings are in full_body_parameters.json. Some accessory
coordinates intentionally match fixed locations in the original rover
design. Changing a parameter is not a guarantee of arbitrary-size fit;
regenerate and review the geometry after modification.
