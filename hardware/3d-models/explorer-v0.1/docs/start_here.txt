WHAT THE MODEL REPRESENTS

The supplied official repository archive contains Bottom Plate.stl,
Top Plate.stl and Dead Plate.stl. It does not contain factory CAD for the
motors, wheels, electronics or complete assembly. The three plates were
reconstructed as solids using sections of those supplied meshes. The
remaining purchased parts are explicitly labeled reference geometry.

Major stock components represented include both chassis levels, the servo
plate, four motors and their mounting hardware, four wheels and hubs, six
pillars, the UNO and expansion shield, connector blocks, the GY-521 module,
IR/RGB/button details, the battery enclosure, the servo and head bracket,
ultrasonic board and transducers, camera PCB/radio/lens, the underside line
sensor board, representative fasteners, and a schematic head-cable route.

This is a complete major-component layout for planning and modification.
Small electronic details, internal battery construction, exact cable
lengths, connectors and individual fastener shapes are simplified. Do not
use purchased-part reference bodies as manufacturing drawings.

The verification/component_manifest.json file records every modeled component's group,
variant, evidence level, notes, dimensions and volume. Evidence categories
distinguish supplied-mesh geometry, documented nominal hardware, nominal
component envelopes, estimated envelopes, and new designs.

Source geometry and known discrepancy:
  - Main plates: approximately 132.523 x 230.780 x 4.5 mm each.
  - Supplied plate gap: 39.705222 mm. This exact gap is preserved, although
    the ELEGOO assembly manual describes nominal M3 x 40 mm pillars.
  - Source Dead Plate: 32.8 x 41.0 x 2.6 mm; its source pose is retained in
    the stock reference file, including the 0.180406 mm gap above the top.
  - The top mesh has a defect where nearby cutouts meet at the servo opening.
    The solid reconstruction produces a valid cutout using sequential cuts.
  - The supplied Dead Plate's three-hole row does not match the Top Plate:
    the row pitches are 15.60/17.65 mm versus approximately 15.15/15.15 mm.
    The stock STEP preserves this discrepancy rather than moving the source
    holes. The explorer STEP shows the separate optional replacement adapter.
  - Outer-profile simplification differs by at most 0.02 mm at the measured
    section vertices. This is not a global accuracy claim for the car.
  - Reconstructed plate volumes differ from the source STL mesh volumes by
    approximately -0.0052% (bottom), -0.0165% (top) and -0.0519% (Dead Plate).

Retain your physical stock servo bracket if it already fits and works. The
optional adapter is useful only after you check its servo opening, ear
holes and the physical top-plate pattern. Its main row follows the source
Top Plate; its servo opening and ear fit are nominal, not measured from
your particular servo.


OPENING AND EDITING IN AUTODESK FUSION

1. Download cad/explorer_assembly.step and use Fusion's local-file opening or
   upload workflow for STEP files. Fusion officially supports STEP import.
2. Save the imported design as a Fusion project and confirm document units
   are millimeters. The supplied STEP files explicitly declare millimeters.
3. Expand the named groups to inspect or hide the source chassis, stock
   references, PLA accessories, optional hardware and added fasteners.
4. For an individual print edit, open cad/printable_parts.step and
   select the relevant component. Keep its mounting pattern fixed unless
   you are intentionally adapting it to measured hardware.
5. Export only the edited printable component as STEP or STL for slicing.

The STEP files contain separated, named BRep solid geometry and assembly
relationships. They are not native F3D files with a sketch/feature timeline,
motion joints, contacts or simulation setup. Fusion direct edits and new
features can be added after import. Source-level parameters are also
provided for regenerating the design outside Fusion.

do not require an AMS or multiple filaments.

There are 24 build objects across the six packs and 13 unique designs.
All packs have been checked within a 180 x 180 mm geometric print area.
The front guard is the largest part at 170 mm wide. With a 4 mm brim around
that part, the width becomes 178 mm. Check the usable area on your selected
printer, and rearrange objects if your brim or slicer exclusions need it.

Suggested starting settings, not a tested printer profile:
  - PLA using the printer's established material profile.
  - 0.4 mm nozzle; 0.20 mm layer height.
  - Four walls and approximately 20-25% gyroid infill.
  - Four or five top/bottom layers where those settings apply.
  - Consider a 4 mm brim for the tall deck spacers and the long front guard.
  - Parts are already oriented in the STLs and 3MFs. Side guards lie on
    their broad outer faces; spacers stand upright. Review the first layer
    and bridges in the sliced preview before deciding on local supports.
  - Use metal through-bolts and nuts. The PLA parts have clearance holes;
    they do not depend on printed threads, flexible latches or heat inserts.

Keep the body ventilated. Avoid locating the PLA directly against a hot
processor or power regulator. All accessories can be printed in one color.


FIT CHECK AND BUILD ORDER

1. Print pack 00: the gauge and all four tall spacers.
2. With the car powered off, replace the four middle/rear top-plate bolts
   one at a time. Put a printed spacer at each location and position the
   gauge on top, 36 mm above the stock top plate.
3. Start the four proposed M3 x 50 mm screws loosely. All four must enter
   the existing pillar threads without forcing the gauge or bending a
   plate. Keep the chassis supported while changing these bolts.
4. Check thread depth before tightening. The 2 mm gauge gives about 7.5 mm
   nominal engagement; the final 3.2 mm deck gives about 6.3 mm. If a screw
   bottoms out, select a shorter screw or suitable washers after measuring.
   Do not use tightening force to compensate for a mismatch.
5. Check the hole-size ladder on the gauge: 3.1, 3.3, 3.5, 3.7 and 3.9 mm
   from left to right in the supplied top-view orientation. The design's
   M3 clearance is 3.5 mm. Adjust hole size locally if your print needs it.
6. Confirm clearance around your battery, controller connectors and cable
   plugs. The gauge verifies the mounting pattern, not every payload fit.
7. Print pack 01 and replace the gauge with the deck on the same spacers.
   Leave the service opening above the controller accessible.
8. Print pack 02 and the two guard shims from pack 03. Fasten the side-panel
   flanges beneath the deck. Mount the front guard on its two 4 mm shims
   above the front top-plate pillars.
   Add soft foam tape to the guard's contact face if desired.
9. Print and fit the remaining required pieces from pack 03. The rear
   sensor carrier bolts to the two rear deck holes. The downward carriers
   bolt below the guard;
   strap the actual sensor boards beneath them with their optics facing
   the floor. Leave optical apertures unobstructed. Cable saddles can use
   suitable deck slots with bolts/nuts or ties; their positions are chosen
   during wiring and are not fixed in the installed assembly.
10. Print pack 04 only if adding a Pi 4 Model B. The tray mounts above the
    deck on four 4 mm spacers. Check board underside/connector access with
    your actual board and cooling arrangement before fastening it.
11. Use pack 05 only if you choose to replace the stock servo bracket after
    comparing it to the physical parts. It is not required for the deck.
12. Check wheel movement, camera/sonar visibility and the full physical head
    motion with cables connected. The model's sweep review covers rigid
    reference shapes; it does not model cable bending or servo hard stops.

If the gauge does not fit, modify the mounting coordinates or local hole
clearances. Do not scale the entire model: that also changes wheel, board,
fastener and accessory interfaces.

large quantity. Lengths below are a starting BOM for the modeled stack.

The optional Pi board stack is approximately 10.4 mm before the nut; check
washer and nut thickness against the actual board. The Pi tray uses the
nominal 85 x 56 mm PCB and 58 x 49 mm hole pitch, offset toward the left
edge of the board. A Pi case, HAT, cooler or different board needs its own
clearance check. The optional servo adapter needs three suitable M3
through-fasteners and the appropriate servo-ear screws; choose lengths
from the actual stack and retain working kit hardware where appropriate.

Also useful: small cable ties, foam tape, and suitable washers where space
allows. Some accessory fasteners in this BOM are not explicitly modeled
(rear carrier, cable saddles and Pi board screws), so inspect those local
clearances during assembly. No electrical supply or wiring design for the
optional computer/sensors is included in this mechanical package.


WHY THESE ADDITIONS

Slotted deck: provides an adaptable platform for future sensors or compute
while retaining a service opening above the controller. Mount a sensor on
the deck only after checking its footprint and keeping its view clear.

Removable side guards: help keep the central wiring contained and make
electronics accessible without a complete shell. The wheel areas remain
open and the body has ventilation windows.

Front guard: provides a replaceable, rigid contact surface. It is not a
bumper switch and cannot tell the controller that a collision occurred.

Rear and downward sensor carriers: let you add sensing coverage as the
autonomous explorer develops. The boards shown are generic envelopes, not
a promise of a particular breakout-board fit. Downward brackets alone do
not provide stair protection; the sensors and stop behavior must be tested.

Optional Pi tray: reserves space for a companion computer without making
it a requirement for the first PLA build. Adding a board does not itself
implement mapping or a Home Assistant connection.

For later mapping work, record measured sensor positions/orientations
after the hardware is installed. Use those measurements to calibrate the
software's sensor-to-rover transforms; reference CAD positions alone are
not a calibration.

VALIDATION PERFORMED

verification/final_validation.json contains the detailed checks and hashes of the CAD
and mesh exports. verification/explorer_fit_review.json contains the assembly review.

Export results:
  - Three STEP files re-import as valid solids with the expected counts,
    dimensions, volume, millimeter units, names and assembly relationships.
  - Stock: 114 components; explorer: 167; printable-only master: 13.
  - All six 3MF packs load in strict lib3mf 2.5.0 with zero warnings.
  - The 24 correctly named build objects are on Z=0 and inside 180 x 180 mm.
  - All 13 individual STL designs pass independent VTK manifoldness,
    connected-surface, orientation, bounds and volume checks.
  - Maximum STL volume deviation from analytic CAD: approximately 0.060%.

Assembly review:
  - No positive-volume intersections above 0.01 mm3 between PLA parts,
    source plates and the purchased-hardware reference envelopes reviewed.
  - No added-fastener-to-PLA/source-plate intersections above that threshold.
  - Smallest reviewed side-guard/deck-spacer gap: approximately 1.667 mm.
  - Side guard to nominal tires: at least 9.705 mm; front guard to nominal
    tires: at least 10.515 mm. Closest accessory-to-tire gap: 6.647 mm.
  - A conservative cylindrical envelope around the modeled rotating head
    stays clear of the printed accessories throughout a +/-90 degree sweep.
    Front-guard clearance is 13.058 mm; overall minimum is 7.000 mm to the
    optional servo adapter. Flexible cable routing is excluded.
  - The mounting gauge clears the modeled hardware at the intended deck
    height on its four spacers.

These checks are digital verification, not a physical fit or print test.
Application-level opening in Fusion/Bambu Studio, slicing, printing,
material strength, payload capacity, minimum wall thickness and complete
mesh self-intersection certification have not been performed. The fit
review depends on the stated approximate hardware dimensions. Its source
hash records the geometry-reviewed generator; the subsequent generator
change only replaced the preview renderer, leaving geometry unchanged.


REBUILDING AND FUTURE DEVELOPMENT

Keep these files together:
  source/build_explorer.py
  source/explorer_parameters.json
  source/stock_profiles.json
  source/rover_3mf.py
  source/render_previews.py        (needed only for previews)
  source/validate_final_exports.py (needed only for independent export validation)
  source/requirements.txt

In a Python 3.11+ environment, install source/requirements.txt, then run:
  python source/build_explorer.py --output rebuilt
  python source/build_explorer.py --output rebuilt --render

To create previews without re-exporting STEP/STL/3MF:
  python source/build_explorer.py --output previews --manifest-only --render

For strict validation of rebuilt exports:
  python source/validate_final_exports.py --deliverables rebuilt

The generator uses CadQuery 2.7.0. Preview rendering uses Matplotlib's
headless Agg backend; it does not require a display server. Validation
also uses VTK (available with the CAD environment) and lib3mf 2.5.0.
source/stock_profiles.json contains the extracted source section geometry, so
the original archive is not required merely to regenerate this model.

source/explorer_parameters.json exposes major envelope/deck settings. Some
interfaces are fixed source-derived coordinates and some component details
are defined in the Python generator. Parameters are not a guarantee of
arbitrary-size configurability: review clearances after changing them.

Every printable design also has an individual, print-oriented STL with the
same component name as in the 3MF pack. Their quantities and dimensions are
listed in verification/component_manifest.json. verification/sha256sums.txt covers the final files.
