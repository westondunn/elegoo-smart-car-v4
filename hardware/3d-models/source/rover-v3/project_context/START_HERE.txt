ROVER V3 — FULL PLA CHASSIS AND FUSION PERSONAL PROJECT
20 October 2026

WHAT YOU HAVE

Rover_V3_Full_X1C_PLA.3mf is the complete printable project. It contains the
full lower base, upper service deck and every required printed support/rail/
spacer, already arranged on two plates. It retains real model geometry and
sliced toolpaths; it is not a picture-only or empty 3MF.

Rover_V3_Fusion_Personal_Project.zip is the editable assembly package. It
contains the portable STEP assembly, a desktop Fusion builder, individual
component solids, native feature recipes, CAD source, project instructions,
electronics references, assembly notes, BOM and validation reports.

Rover_V3_Complete_Assembly.step is also supplied separately for immediate
geometry import. STEP carries solid geometry and assembly structure; the
Fusion builder adds the native materials, features and joints.

The final native .f3d is produced by running the builder in desktop Fusion.
Fusion has not been run in this environment; a file has not merely been
renamed to .f3d. See README_FUSION.txt inside the package for the exact steps.

OPENING AND USING THE PROJECT WITH HANDY

1. Open the .3mf as a PROJECT in desktop Bambu Studio. Keep its printer,
   filament/process presets, all plates and all objects.
2. Confirm the actual nozzle, PLA and physical build plate. If your plate is
   not textured PEI, select the actual plate type and reslice both plates.
3. Review both plates in Prepare and Preview. If any setting is changed or
   Studio requests slicing, choose Slice All.
4. Use Studio's Send All command to transfer the full multi-plate job to your
   paired printer. Select the filament mapping in the transfer dialog.
5. Select each plate from the printer's stored job/file controls. Handy may
   also expose the transferred job through its printer/file controls depending
   on app and firmware. Desktop Studio is the initial custom-file transfer path.
6. Remove each completed print and prepare the bed before starting the next.

The exact delivered file is exported and reopened by native Bambu Studio.
A phone-local import into Handy has not been tested, and no job has been sent
to a printer or published to MakerWorld while preparing this package.

WHAT CHANGED MECHANICALLY

The main body is widened by translating the original motor/wheel-relief
geometry outward on each side, then adding the central span. The original
front/rear asymmetry is retained. It is not a uniformly scaled stock chassis.
Each of the four local motor interfaces uses the exact C3 bores, bearing
floor, washer pocket and rim derived from the successful uploaded C1 part.

Motor pockets are 7.4 mm wide and 3.3 mm deep; the tested 1.8 mm bearing floor
is retained. Your 4 mm diameter x 2 mm high heads and 6 mm diameter washers
therefore have the selected recess allowance. Stock motor screw thread size
has not been inferred from its head diameter.

The flat battery remains centered and low. Its measured body is
69.85 x 146.05 x 12.7 mm. The rail bay is 73.025 x 149.225 mm, adding 1.5875 mm
(1/16 inch) on each side/end. The open centers leave room for connectors;
check actual plug access before tightening. The corner stops leave a
61.025 mm central opening. Four base slots accept two 12 mm hook-and-loop
straps for vertical retention.

The motor battery is on the rear upper service deck. That moves its plug,
switch and charging-port area above the motors and away from the lower Pi
post/axle conflict. The integrated adjustment mount retains 65–90 mm hole
spacing, with a continuous 7.4 mm-wide x 3.3 mm-deep head/washer recess and
2.4 mm bearing floor. 76 mm is the original nominal pitch, not a claim that
your pack has that exact spacing. Start with the switch facing rear and
verify that both actual connectors can fully seat before tightening.

The Pi and UNO are side by side on removable 6 mm standoffs above the deck.
The deck has six supports rather than permanent board risers near an axle.
The original front head interface and line-tracker hole pattern are retained.
Cable windows and spare slots provide future attachment and routing points.

HARDWARE TO HAVE READY

Reuse the working motors, blocks, shafts, wheels, their tested fasteners,
the motor-battery fasteners, complete front head/bracket, electronics and
line-tracker hardware. The longer deck supports require additional hardware:

  Deck supports: 6 x M3x50 low/button hex screws, 6 x M3 nuts, 12 x 6 mm OD washers.
  Battery rails: 6 x M3x8 low/button hex screws, 6 x M3 nuts, 6 x 6 mm OD washers.
  Pi: 4 x M2.5x16 screws, 4 matching nuts, 8 thin washers appropriate to the PCB.
  UNO: 3 x M3x16 screws, 3 matching nuts, 6 thin washers appropriate to the PCB.
  Powerbank: 2 hook-and-loop straps, 12 mm wide, approximately 220–250 mm long.

The new low-head screw recommendation assumes a maximum 2 mm head height,
0.5 mm-thick washers and standard nominal nut heights (M3: 2.4 mm;
M2.5: 2 mm). The Pi and UNO screw lengths also assume 1.6 mm-thick PCBs.
Match actual hardware to the stack. A 6 mm washer diameter does not establish
washer thickness.

Use 50 mm support screws, not 55 mm: with the stated washer/nut stack, the
50 mm tips finish 2.4 mm below the nominal board underside. Longer screws
could project into the board area. Actual solder joints/underside components
are not modeled. Use snug, even tightening so the PLA seats flat.

Motor-battery lug thickness and original thread type are unmeasured. Reuse
matching stock screws where suitable; select any replacement by the actual
lug, 2.4 mm deck bearing floor, washer and nut/thread engagement. The separate
hardware_and_assembly.json records the complete calculated stacks.

ASSEMBLY ORDER

1. Find FRONT on the lower base. The broad flat motor-contact face points up
   when installed; the motor head/washer recesses point down. The supplied
   print orientation is the reverse because it exposes those recesses upward.
2. Insert the six rail nuts into the underside hex pockets. Install both rails
   on top, rotating one rail 180 degrees so their end stops face inward.
3. Thread the two straps through the four base slots. Fit the centered power
   bank, check its connector access, and close the straps without squeezing it.
4. Install all four stock motor/block/wheel groups using their matching front
   and rear hole pairs. Keep the proven stock group geometry intact.
5. Fit the line tracker underneath with the 3 mm spacers or the stock spacers
   appropriate to the real sensor-to-ground distance.
6. Install the six 40.5 mm support posts. Prepare the upper deck's battery,
   board standoffs and original front head while both sides remain accessible.
7. Set the motor-battery slot to its actual mounting pitch. Route the existing
   wiring through the service windows, then fasten the deck with the 50 mm
   screws and stated washer/nut stack. Keep cables clear of shafts and wheels.
8. Tighten all six deck-support nuts before installing the Pi and UNO on their
   6 mm standoffs. The support at X48, Y0 is below the UNO envelope; installing
   that board first obstructs access to its support nut.
9. With power off and the rover lifted, turn each wheel through a full turn.
   Check screw tips and actual plugs before first operation. The local motor
   fit is retained; the new full assembly has not yet been physically printed.

FUSION CONTENTS AND EDITING

The assembly contains 64 named components and 63 joint definitions:
  58 rigid joints for installed parts.
  4 revolute wheel joints.
  1 revolute front-head joint with nominal -80 to +80 degree limits.

The builder creates 17 dimension-driven native post/spacer components using
sketches, extrusions and bores. It imports the complex base, deck, bank rails
and reference hardware as BRep solids. If a Fusion API/version rejects a
native recipe, it falls back to that part's exact STEP and records the fallback.
Named parameters distinguish actual driving dimensions from reference values.
Changing a post height alone does not automatically reposition the imported
deck and electronics; review the assembly datums when changing its height.

Materials and display colors are separate. The builder assigns matching
physical materials found in Fusion's loaded libraries, records unavailable
materials, and sets part colors. For missing PLA, follow the custom-material
instructions in README_FUSION.txt using your filament's density. The selected
Bambu Generic PLA profile uses 1.24 g/cm3 as a nominal density reference.
Infill, composite boards, batteries and hollow hardware mean that solid-CAD
mass is not a calibrated assembled mass or center-of-gravity prediction.

Electronic boards, connectors, motor bodies, wheels and the scanning head
are labelled reference envelopes where exact components are not identified.
The Pi model is unconfirmed. Wheel axes are illustrative placements within
the stock relief areas, not reverse-engineered shaft measurements. Replace
these reference solids with measured/manufacturer models before relying on
motion sweeps for physical clearance. The measured battery/block dimensions
and source-backed mounting patterns are recorded separately.

The original ELEGOO schematic PDFs and newer repository reference are
included. The 2020 shield schematic and newer firmware differ in motor-driver
pin assignments; keep your working wiring intact. There is no fabricated PCB
layout, automatically rewired electronics, electrical simulation or stress
simulation in this package. Relevant Personal features are documented in
README_FUSION.txt with official sources and any edition/API limitations.

VALIDATION PERFORMED

- All 21 printed STEP instances are valid single solids and round-trip through
  the CAD kernel. All 7 unique printable meshes are closed single components,
  consistently oriented, with no degenerate triangles.
- Boolean comparison reports zero change in all four C3 motor-interface
  neighborhoods. The battery-slot 2.4 mm bearing floor was probed in the CAD.
- All 210 printed-part pairs were screened for interference; no positive-volume
  printed-to-printed overlap was found. The measured bank and nominal hardware
  envelopes also have no volume intersections with printed parts in the shown
  installed pose. That limited envelope check does not establish actual fit.
- Native Bambu export/reopen, plate membership, retained geometry, extrusions
  for every object, printer metadata, bed/cutter limits and profiles are audited.
- Fusion input files, script syntax, reference volumes/bounds and joint graph
  are validated offline. The combined STEP is reopened and checked separately.
- Physical printing, load/vibration testing, actual Handy phone import and
  native Fusion runtime/joint-health execution remain unperformed here.
